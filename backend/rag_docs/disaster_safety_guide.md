# Disaster Safety Quick Guide (sample document for the demo)

This is a short, general-purpose sample so the RAG demo works out of the box.
Replace or add official documents (NDMA, SDMA, Red Cross, WHO) in this folder, or upload them from the chatbot page.

Emergency numbers in India: 112 is the national emergency number. 1070 is the state disaster control room helpline and 1077 is the district collector control room. 108 is ambulance. 101 is fire. 100 is police.

## Floods

Before a flood: keep an emergency kit with drinking water, dry food, a torch, a power bank, a first-aid kit, important documents in a waterproof bag, and essential medicines. Know the nearest relief shelter and the route to higher ground. Switch off the main electric supply if water may enter the house.

During a flood: move immediately to higher ground. Do not walk, swim or drive through floodwater, because even 15 cm of moving water can knock a person down and 60 cm can carry away a vehicle. Stay away from electric poles and fallen wires. Drink only boiled or bottled water. Avoid touching floodwater because it may contain sewage and chemicals.

After a flood: return home only when authorities declare it safe. Check for structural damage before entering. Throw away food that touched floodwater. Clean and disinfect everything that got wet. Watch for snakes and insects that may have entered the house. Report waterborne illness symptoms such as diarrhoea, fever and vomiting to a doctor early.

## Cyclones and Strong Winds

Before a cyclone: secure loose objects, trim weak tree branches, and keep a battery radio for official warnings. Fill vehicles with fuel and charge all phones. Fishermen should not go to sea after a warning.

During a cyclone: stay indoors, away from windows and glass. Shelter in the strongest part of the building, such as an inner room on a lower floor. Do not go outside during the calm eye of the storm, because strong winds return from the opposite direction. Keep listening to official updates.

After a cyclone: stay away from fallen power lines and damaged buildings. Do not drink tap water until it is declared safe. Use torches instead of candles to avoid fires.

## Earthquakes

During shaking: drop, cover and hold on. Get under a sturdy table and protect your head and neck. If outdoors, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, bridges and electric wires. If driving, stop in an open place and stay inside the vehicle. Do not use lifts.

After shaking: expect aftershocks. Check yourself and others for injuries and give first aid. Leave damaged buildings carefully. Turn off gas and electricity if you smell gas or see damage. Do not light matches or use lighters if you suspect a gas leak.

## Fire

If there is a fire: raise the alarm and call 101. Leave the building immediately. Crawl low under smoke because cleaner air is near the floor. Touch a closed door with the back of your hand before opening it; if it is hot, do not open it. Never use a lift. Stop, drop and roll if clothes catch fire. Once outside, stay outside.

For an electrical fire, do not use water. Switch off the power supply if it is safe to do so and use a dry powder or CO2 extinguisher.

## First Aid Basics

Severe bleeding: apply firm direct pressure with a clean cloth and keep the injured part raised if possible. Do not remove the cloth if it soaks through; add more on top. Call 108.

Unconscious but breathing: place the person on their side in the recovery position and keep the airway clear. Call 108 immediately.

Not breathing: call 108 and start chest compressions: push hard and fast in the centre of the chest at about 100 to 120 compressions per minute until help arrives.

Burns: cool the burn with clean running water for at least 20 minutes. Do not apply ice, toothpaste or butter. Cover loosely with a clean cloth.

Drowning rescue: do not enter the water unless trained. Throw a rope, pole or floating object. After rescue, if the person is not breathing, call 108 and begin CPR.

## Landslides

Warning signs include cracks in the ground, tilting trees or poles, sudden muddy streams, and rumbling sounds. Move away from the slide path at right angles, not downhill. Avoid river valleys and steep slopes during heavy rain. Report blocked roads and unstable slopes to the authorities.

## Shelter and Evacuation Tips

Carry identity documents, medicines, phone with charger, torch, water and dry food. Help children, elderly people, pregnant women and people with disabilities first. Lock the house and switch off electricity and gas. Follow the evacuation routes announced by the authorities and register at the shelter so family members can find you. Keep emergency contacts written on paper in case phones die.
